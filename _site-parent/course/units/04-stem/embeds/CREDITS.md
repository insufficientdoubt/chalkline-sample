# Credits: the STEM unit's interactive pages

The pages in this folder (`blocks.html`, `triangle.html`, `linkage.html`, `lifts.html`, `catapult.html`, `parabola.html`) were written for this course; `lifts.html` and `catapult.html` are plain SVG and JavaScript, with no library. They use:

| What | Used in | Licence | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Blockly** 11.2.1 | `blocks.html` | Apache 2.0 (`blockly/NOTICE.txt`) | Google; saved here from the npm package `blockly` |
| **JSXGraph** 1.10.1 | `triangle.html`, `linkage.html` | MIT or LGPL (`jsxgraph/LICENSE.MIT`, `LICENSE.LGPL`) | University of Bayreuth; saved here from the npm package `jsxgraph` |
| **GeoGebra** | `parabola.html` | free for non-commercial use | loaded live from geogebra.org (needs the internet) |

The slides also show two outside sites in a frame, loaded live (they need the internet):

- **PhET Interactive Simulations**, University of Colorado Boulder, CC BY 4.0: *Graphing Lines*.
- **Blockly Games** (Google, Apache 2.0): *Maze*.

The linkage presets follow well-known designs: Hoeken's straight-line linkage (link lengths 1 : 2 : 2.5 : 2.5, with the path point 5 along the coupler) and Grashof's rule for four-bar linkages.
