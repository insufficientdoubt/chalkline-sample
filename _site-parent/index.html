What we’re learning

Start Here: What Chalkline Does

A short slide deck about Chalkline: what it makes for you, and how this sample course is put together.

  • I can find the slides, printables, packets and vocab for a unit.
  • I can explain what Chalkline makes from one file (slides, web page, PDF).
  • I know where to start when I make my own unit.
1

Unit 1: Ancient Greece

How geography shaped the Greek city-states, how democracy worked in Athens, and the Greek ideas we still use today.

  • I can explain how mountains and the sea led to many separate city-states.
  • I can compare life and government in Athens and Sparta.
  • I can describe how Athenian democracy worked, and who could and couldn't vote.
  • I can name Greek ideas we still use today, from the Olympics to columns and theater.
2

Unit 2: Ancient Rome

How Rome went from a republic to an empire, and the roads, aqueducts, buildings and words it left behind.

  • I can explain how the Roman Republic was governed, and how it was different from Athens.
  • I can describe how Rome became an empire under Augustus.
  • I can explain how an aqueduct uses gravity to move water.
  • I can read and write Roman numerals and find Latin in English words.
3

Unit 3: Göbekli Tepe

The oldest known monument in the world: giant carved stones raised by hunter-gatherers 11,500 years ago, and how archaeologists use evidence to work out what it was.

  • I can say where and when Göbekli Tepe was built, and how old it is compared with Greece and Rome.
  • I can describe the T-pillars, the enclosures and the animal carvings.
  • I can explain how hunter-gatherers could cut and move huge stones without metal tools.
  • I can tell evidence from a guess, and explain why archaeologists changed their minds about Göbekli Tepe.
4

Unit 4: STEM Sampler

A sampler of interactive math, coding and engineering: geometry and graphs you can drag, block coding that turns into Python, robot lifts and catapults that move, and practice worksheets.

  • I can explain why the angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
  • I can describe how a, b and c change the graph of y = ax² + bx + c.
  • I can build a short program with blocks and read the same program in Python.
  • I can explain how a parallelogram keeps a robot's claw level, and compare a 4-bar, a 6-bar and a DR4B lift.
  • I can describe three ways to pull back and release a rubber-band catapult.
